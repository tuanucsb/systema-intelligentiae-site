The big question · six formulas, one census

Has Earth become more intelligent?

It depends on what you count. The census measures energy, events and memory at 64 moments in Earth’s history, and it never adds them into one score. This page tries, six different ways: two formulas from the physics of energy, three from information theory, and one that joins them. They do not agree, and where they disagree is the interesting part.

What the six formulas say

These formulas are proposals built on top of the census, not results from it. They describe the whole planet over time. None of them ranks a species, a person or a machine.

What the census counts

Each chart has one point per moment, not one per year, so the last points cover only the last 5,000 years. Each step up the side of a chart is ten times more unless it says otherwise. Red triangles mark mass extinctions and freezes. Point at any chart, or use the slider, to read a moment in all of them.

Approach 1 · thermodynamics

Count the energy

Thinking of any kind uses energy, and physics sets a lowest price. In 1961 Rolf Landauer showed that erasing one bit must release a small, fixed amount of heat that depends only on temperature. So a planet’s power, divided by that price, is the most information work it could ever pay for.

Approach 2 · information theory

Count the information

In 1948 Claude Shannon gave information a unit, the bit, and two rules that matter here. A channel is measured by how many bits pass each second. A store is measured by what is new in it: a second copy of the same message adds nothing.

Approach 3 · both together

Join the two: energy in, knowledge out

The two approaches meet in one chain. Power sets a budget. Efficiency turns part of the budget into events. A tiny part of those events leaves something new behind, and what is left behind adds up to what the planet knows. The last formula follows that chain from end to end.

Bring every ingredient together

The six formulas each leave something out. This mixer does not: give each ingredient a weight and it adds up their digit counts. A weight of 1 counts an ingredient’s digits once, 2 counts them twice, 0 leaves it out. The choice of weights is a judgement, not a measurement, so try several.

What these formulas cannot say

  • The census keeps its quantities apart on purpose. A reaction in a cell, a nerve signal and a chip operation are different kinds of event. Adding them, as Doing does, treats them as equal.
  • One bit per event is an assumption. It sets the level of Efficiency and Learning share. The direction of each line does not depend on it.
  • Outside memory still counts copies. Knowing would be lower, and its crossing with DNA later, with a de-duplicated count that the census does not have.
  • Learning is measured between neighbouring moments. The gaps run from years to , so each value is an average over its gap.
  • None of this measures understanding. The formulas count energy, events and stored bits. Whether a stored bit is true or useful is outside them.
