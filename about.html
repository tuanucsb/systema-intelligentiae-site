About

A natural history of thinking matter

Systema Intelligentiae tells how Earth learned to think: how memory, wiring and computing were built up over 4.1 billion years, from chemistry in a rock pore to today’s data centers. Every page draws on one open census, so the story, the tree and the data always agree.

How it was made

  1. 68 moments & 10 parts. Earth’s history is split into 68 moments, from 4.1 billion years ago to 2026—all 68 are stops on the Journey (with a 26-stop quick tour), connected across 10 functional parts and 173 inventions on the Tree.
  2. Volume vs. single-system complexity. Each moment tracks ten planet-wide quantities separately—never added into one score—while the 703-system Atlas and time-scrubbable pie & complexity map compare one individual cell, tree, animal or supercomputer against planet totals.
  3. Deep-time tracks & book lenses. Dedicated dated ledgers trace DNA & genomes, sexual reproduction, plants & fungi, immune systems, molecular motors, neural circuits, communication networks, replication & manufacturing and off-world spacecraft, paired with three landmark book lenses (Schrödinger, Bennett, Agüera y Arcas).
  4. Sources & honest uncertainty. Each number traces to published estimates in the Data Lab. Today’s values carry 95% ranges from a 10,000-draw Monte Carlo; older moments carry explicit fossil, gene-clock or living-relative evidence tags (how sure are we?).
  5. Checked twice. A validator checks the census, milestone dates, invention links and image credits against each other every time the site is built.
