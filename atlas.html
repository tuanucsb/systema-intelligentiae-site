Atlas of minds · individual system complexity & catalog

Every system we track, one unit at a time

Planet totals multiply population by speed, so trillions of simple bacteria outweigh brains on raw volume. Here each dot and row measures one individual system—one bacterium, one spruce tree, one honeybee, one human brain, one clay archive or one AI cluster—so you can see how much information a single system processes and browse the full database behind this site.

Complexity over deep time

Vertical height shows operations per second inside one unit; horizontal position shows when that kind of system first appeared. Click a category pill to filter both the map and the database below, or click any dot to jump to its row.

Both axes step by ×10. One grid line higher means ten times more operations a second in a single unit; one grid line left means ten times further back in time.

Calculation rules & scaling

How one unit’s operations per second is calculated

Chemical turnovers, nerve signals, archive writes and chip cycles are different physical events and are never summed into one planetary score. To place individual systems on a common order-of-magnitude scale, each group converts published anatomical or hardware measurements into events per second using its own physical equation.
